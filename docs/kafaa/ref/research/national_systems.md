# National Skills Systems as Benchmarks for Kafa'a (ENEC)

Research date: 2026-10-10. Theme: **Future Skills and Competencies**.
Every item below carries source URL + publication/update date. Where a figure could not be
confirmed against a primary source, it is marked **[UNVERIFIED]**.

Context for "what Kafa'a could borrow": ENEC operates Barakah (4 × APR-1400), is standing up the
ADVANCE programme for SMRs/advanced reactors, and faces KHNP operator-support wind-down around
2030. That means the live problems are: (a) forecasting which competencies will be needed when,
(b) proving/transferring competence as KHNP staff hand over, (c) giving Emirati staff a credible,
visible route from "operator today" to "SMR/advanced-reactor role in 2032", and (d) tying all of
it to recognised national/international qualifications rather than internal badges only.

---

## 1. SINGAPORE — SkillsFuture (the deepest benchmark; owner: SkillsFuture Singapore / MOM / WSG)

Singapore is the single most transferable model for Kafa'a because it has built the full stack:
a competency taxonomy → labour-market telemetry → a personal verified record → personalised
recommendation → funded entitlement → sector-level job redesign. Kafa'a can mirror that stack
inside one employer.

### 1.1 Skills Frameworks (SSG, Singapore, 2016–present; current scope confirmed 2025/26)

**What it is.** 38 sector Skills Frameworks covering ~2,000 job roles, each role decomposed into
key work tasks and into Technical Skills & Competencies (TSCs) plus Critical Core Skills (CCS).
SSG's 2025 factsheet states the frameworks hold **about 37,000 key work tasks across 2,000 job
roles in the 38 Skills Frameworks**.

**Mechanism users see.** For any job role: a role description, the tasks, the skill list with
proficiency levels, the adjacent roles you can move to, and the courses mapped to each skill.
Skills are grouped into *skill clusters* ("Strategic Business Management", "Engineering and
Production", "IT and Data Management", "Operation Excellence", "Service Excellence",
"Organisation and People Management") formed by grouping skills on content and refined by domain
experts.

**Measurable results.** SSG publishes *in-demand* vs *growth* skills per cluster from online job
posting data. For 2025, in IT & Data Management the top five **growth** skills were all generative
AI (Generative AI Principles and Applications; GenAI Models Technical Aspects of Security and
Ethics; GenAI Model Evaluation; Responsible AI and GenAI Practices; GenAI Model Selection), while
the top five **in-demand** skills were still traditional (Infrastructure Support, Applications
Development, Performance Management, IT Standards, Software Design). Demand for AI-related
capabilities **more than doubled from 2022 to 2025 across sectors**.

- Source (PDF, primary, verified by direct download): SSG, *Factsheet 2 — Top Skills Trends in 2025*, https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/41/4955bc61-161d-4584-945c-7eeeb276867a/Factsheet%20on%20Top%20skills%20trends%20in%202025.pdf (published with the Skills Demand for the Future Economy report cycle, 2025/26)
- Source: SSG newsroom, *SkillsFuture Singapore tightens course funding guidelines and identifies skills trends*, https://www.ssg.gov.sg/newsroom/skillsfuture-singapore-tightens-course-funding-guidelines-and-identifies-skills-trends-to-strengthen-workforce-development/

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. **A Barakah/ADVANCE Skills Framework object model**: role → key work tasks → TSC with 1–5
   proficiency → linked learning asset → adjacent roles. Kafa'a already has competency data; the
   missing piece is the *task layer* between role and skill, which is what makes AI-impact and
   job-redesign analysis possible at all.
2. **Skill clusters as a navigation spine** (e.g. Reactor Operations, Nuclear Safety & Licensing,
   Maintenance & Outage, Fuel & Chemistry, Radiation Protection, Digital/I&C, Emergency
   Preparedness, Leadership) so every dashboard, search and report rolls up the same way.
3. **"In-demand vs growth skills" split**, computed from ENEC's own vacancy/requisition and
   project data, so a user sees both *what is hired for now* and *what is accelerating*.

### 1.2 "AI Potential on Tasks" dashboard + role archetypes (SSG, Singapore, 2025)

**What it is.** Two pilot studies at the *work-task* level, published as a live dashboard on the
Jobs-Skills Portal.

**Mechanism.** Study 1 analysed ~37,000 key work tasks across 2,000 roles and scored each for AI
automation/augmentation potential; SSG then published an **"AI Potential on Tasks" dashboard**
showing AI potential across job roles "enabling more informed decisions on training development,
job redesign and workforce planning". Study 2 covered six sectors and classified entry roles into
three archetypes:
- **Mastery Builders** (e.g. Accounting Executive) — progressive mastery; risk is that AI
  automates the foundational tasks juniors used to learn on, cutting the rung off the ladder.
- **Analytical Specialists** (e.g. E-Commerce Associate) — front-loaded learning; emphasis shifts
  to judgment and interpretation.
- **Human Connectors** (e.g. Customer Service Executive) — interpersonal skills stay critical.

**Measurable results.** **~24% of tasks** (close to a quarter) "can be significantly aided by the
application of AI" with today's technology. The study also identified a wide range of
*less automatable, enduring* tasks where decision-making, self-management and learning agility
become more valuable as processes get automated.

- Source: same SSG Factsheet 2 PDF above (2025), paragraphs 4–6.

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. **An "AI & automation exposure" score per task and per role**, surfaced to the employee as
   "here's what the tool will do, here's what you'll still own", not as a threat score. Nuclear is
   a strong case: procedure-driven documentation, surveillance scheduling, work-order triage and
   outage planning are high-exposure; licensed operator judgment, FME discipline, emergency
   response and regulator-facing argumentation are low-exposure.
2. **Role archetypes for ENEC** ("Mastery Builder" fits licensed operator and maintenance
   technician paths almost exactly) with an explicit *ladder-rung protection* alert: if AI removes
   the junior tasks that build RO/SRO judgment, the system flags that the progression path needs
   synthetic practice (simulator time, shadow authorisations) to replace lost on-job reps.
3. **A workforce-planning view for HR** that aggregates task-level exposure to answer "which 200
   roles change most by 2030" rather than "which courses were completed".

### 1.3 Critical Core Skills — CCS (SSG, Singapore, established 2020, reviewed 2025)

**What it is.** 16 transferable competencies grouped into Thinking Critically / Interacting with
Others / Staying Relevant, each with proficiency descriptors. 2025 review by SSG + university
researchers concluded that mental wellbeing and career health are best embedded as *outcomes
within* existing skills (Self-Management, Learning Agility) rather than added as new competencies.

**Measurable results.** SSG states employers show strong continuing demand for CCS such as
problem-solving, collaboration and communication, and that these "are even more important in the
age of AI as they complement AI's technical capabilities".

- Source: SSG Factsheet 2 (2025), para 6, URL above.
- Source: 2025 mixed-methods review, https://papers.iafor.org/wp-content/uploads/papers/pcah2025/PCAH2025_93970.pdf (2025)

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. A **nuclear-flavoured core-skills set** (~12–16) that every role carries alongside technical
   competencies: questioning attitude, conservative decision-making, procedural adherence +
   knowing when to stop, error prevention, teamwork/peer-checking, communication under stress,
   learning agility, cross-cultural working (critical for the KHNP→ENEC handover). These map
   cleanly to INPO/WANO human-performance behaviours, so the framework has external grounding.
2. **Don't add a separate "wellbeing" competency** — follow the 2025 finding and treat wellbeing
   and career health as measured outcomes of Self-Management, which avoids competency bloat.

### 1.4 Careers & Skills Passport — CSP (SSG + MOM + MOE + GovTech, Singapore, launched Nov 2024)

**What it is.** A personal digital repository of government-verified career and skills records,
with consented sharing to employers and job portals. **This is the single most directly copyable
feature for Kafa'a.**

**Mechanism — four sections, all verified at source:**
1. **Employment** — employer name, employment period, occupation, verified from CPF Board and MOM.
2. **Skills** — verified skills from certifications *and from employer validation*; individuals
   may also self-add skills (visibly distinguished).
3. **Academic Qualifications** — verified from Singapore Examinations and Assessment Board and the
   Institutes of Higher Learning (ITE, polytechnics, autonomous universities, arts institutions).
4. **Professional Certifications** — WSQ and other SSG-funded course records; self-added records
   allowed to enrich the passport.

Consent is required at three distinct gates: (i) creating the account, (ii) including/accessing
employment data, (iii) sharing curated records to a portal or employer. Users **curate** which
verified records to share and can generate a **shareable public profile link**. The CSP also
serves as a planning tool with occupational and skills insights to identify gaps and receive
personalised training recommendations.

**Measurable results (primary, from the government factsheet).** As of **30 April 2025, 315,000
Singaporeans had accessed their CSP** and **4,900 had shared CSP data** with partnering portals or
employers. **About 30 public and private entities** have adopted or will adopt the CSP. Partner
list includes Jobstreet, FastJobs, Institute of Banking and Finance, Singapore Computer Society,
Institute for Human Resource Professionals, ITE, all polytechnics, NUS/NTU/SMU/SIT and others.
Follow-on: **SEEK Pass** launched 11 Feb 2025 (MOM + SSG + Jobstreet by SEEK) letting candidates
draw on their CSP to show verified credentials to employers.

Note the honest ratio: 315,000 accessed vs 4,900 shared (≈1.6%). Viewing is easy; sharing needs a
reason. Design for the *sharing* moment, not the *viewing* moment.

- Source (PDF, primary, verified by direct download): SSG/MOM, *Celebrating a Decade of SkillsFuture — Annex A: Factsheet on Career & Skills Passport*, https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/41/eb493a0a-f657-4437-b95e-9639a6b0166a/Celebrating%20a%20Decade%20of%20SkillsFuture%20media%20release%20-%20Annex%20A-C.pdf (2025)
- Portal: https://www.myskillsfuture.gov.sg/csp
- SEEK Pass: https://www.hcamag.com/asia/specialisation/hr-technology/new-credential-verification-tool-offered-to-boost-career-health-sg/524324 (Feb 2025)

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. **A "Kafa'a Passport"** with the same four-section split and the same *provenance badge* on
   every row: `ENEC-verified` (from HRIS/training records/licensing system), `Regulator-verified`
   (FANR licence status), `Employer-validated` (line-manager attestation), `Self-declared`. The
   provenance badge is what makes the record trustworthy to a third party — and in nuclear,
   provenance *is* the product.
2. **Three-gate consent + curation before sharing**, including a shareable link with an expiry,
   so an employee moving from Barakah O&M to the ADVANCE SMR programme (or to a vendor/partner
   secondment) can hand over a verified competence profile without HR doing it manually.
3. **A partner-adoption programme**: get FANR, Khalifa University, ADNOC/TAQA/Masdar, the
   National Qualifications Centre and 2–3 vendors to *accept* the passport. A passport nobody
   accepts is a PDF. Singapore's lesson is that the ~30 accepting entities are the feature.

### 1.5 CareersFinder (WSG/MOM, Singapore, launched July 2023)

**What it is.** A personalised jobs-and-skills recommender embedded in MyCareersFuture.

**Mechanism.** Short quiz + government data on **skills adjacencies and job transitions** →
suggests suitable jobs based on your skills and experience, plus training mapped to your career
goals. Draws on MyCareersFuture postings, the Skills Framework and MySkillsFuture records.

**Measurable results.** **More than 55,000 individuals had used CareersFinder as of March 2025**
(since July 2023 launch).

- Source: MOM, *COS 2025 Factsheet on Career Health*, https://www.iac.gov.sg/-/media/mom/documents/budget2025/cos-2025-factsheet-on-career-health.pdf (Mar 2025)
- Source: WSG, *Factsheet on CareersFinder*, https://www.wsg.gov.sg/home/media-room/media-releases/factsheet-on-careersfinder (Mar 2023)

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. A **skills-adjacency graph for ENEC roles** — "from Field Operator you are 3 skills away from
   Reactor Operator, 5 from Chemistry Technician, 7 from I&C Engineer" — with the named gap
   skills and the specific course/simulator/OJT that closes each. Adjacency is computable from the
   existing competency matrix; it is far more motivating than a static org-chart ladder.
2. **Transition evidence, not just aspiration**: show how many ENEC colleagues actually made each
   move and the median time, so recommendations are grounded in real internal mobility history.

### 1.6 Career Health SG + the ACE framework (MOM, Singapore, launched 10 July 2025)

**What it is.** A national reframing of upskilling as ongoing "career health" rather than
crisis-driven retraining, launched at the inaugural Career Health Summit 2025.

**Mechanism.** The **ACE** framework: **A**ssess your career health → **C**hart your career path →
**E**xecute your career plan. Supported by AI jobs/skills insights, career guidance, and
employment facilitation; a one-stop Career Health SG site serves both workers and employers. At
COS 2025, SkillsFuture Credit was extended to **career guidance services** from WSG-endorsed
providers — i.e. the state will fund *advice*, not only courses.

- Source: MOM, *Speech by Minister at Career Health Summit 2025*, https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/speeches/2025/0710-speech-by-min-at-career-health-summit-2025 (10 Jul 2025)
- Source: WSG media release, https://www.wsg.gov.sg/home/media-room/media-releases-speeches/investing-in-career-health-is-indispensable-to-business-success (10 Jul 2025)

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. A **Career Health Score** per employee — a small composite of skills currency (are your
   competencies in-date?), skill breadth vs. role concentration, recency of learning, internal
   mobility optionality, and exposure to automation — reviewed at the annual cycle. One number
   that opens a conversation, with the five components always visible so it never feels like a
   secret rating.
2. **Fund the conversation, not just the course**: an entitlement to N career-guidance sessions
   per year with a trained ENEC career coach, booked inside Kafa'a, with notes feeding the plan.
3. The **ACE three-step spine** as the portal's primary IA: Assess / Chart / Execute. It gives
   every feature an obvious home and stops the portal becoming an LMS with extra tabs.

### 1.7 SkillsFuture Level-Up Programme (MOE/SSG, Singapore, announced Budget 2024; effective 1 May 2024)

**What it is.** A mid-career entitlement for Singaporeans aged 40+: a **S$4,000 non-expiring
SkillsFuture Credit (Mid-Career)** top-up, plus (from 1 May 2025) a **training allowance** of 50%
of average income over the last 12 months, capped at **S$3,000/month, for up to 24 months in a
lifetime**, for selected full-time courses.

**Mechanism.** Crucially the credit is **restricted to a curated list of courses with better
employability outcomes** — roughly **7,000 courses**, of which **~700 full-time programmes**
qualify for the training allowance. That is an outcomes-filtered catalogue, not an open one.

**Measurable results.** First year (1 May 2024 – 30 Apr 2025): **over 36,000 Singapore Citizens
used the mid-career credit, with claims over S$24 million**. Budgeted cost ~S$100M/year.

- Source: MOE, *Career impact of SkillsFuture Credits, mid-career training allowance and Level-Up Programme* (parliamentary reply), https://www.moe.gov.sg/news/parliamentary-replies/20250925-career-impact-of-skillsfuture-credits-mid-career-training-allowance-and-level-up-programme (25 Sep 2025)
- Source: MOE press release, https://www.moe.gov.sg/news/press-releases/20240304-new-skillsfuture-level-up-programme-to-boost-mid-career-upskilling-and-reskilling (4 Mar 2024)
- Source: MOF Budget 2024 Annex D1, https://www.mof.gov.sg/docs/librariesprovider3/budget2024/download/pdf/annexd1.pdf (Feb 2024)

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. An **ENEC learning entitlement wallet** with a visible balance (AED or hours), explicitly
   topped up for mid-career staff (the 15-to-20-years-at-Barakah cohort who will carry the plant
   past 2030) — and **restricted to an outcomes-curated catalogue**, not "any Udemy course".
2. **Paid study time as a first-class object**, not an informal manager favour: a capped,
   policy-backed allowance of protected hours visible in the wallet, requested and approved in
   the portal, with the approval rules written down. Singapore's design insight is that money for
   fees without time to study produces enrolment, not completion.
3. Publish **course-level employability/outcome data** inside the catalogue so the curation is
   legible: completion rate, assessment pass rate, % who moved role within 12 months.

### 1.8 Jobs Transformation Maps — JTMs (WSG, Singapore, ongoing; status as at Nov 2025)

**What it is.** Sector-level studies of how AI, automation, digitalisation and sustainability
change specific jobs, and what the redesigned job and reskilling pathway looks like.

**Mechanism.** Each JTM identifies key technologies driving change, the impact on **individual job
roles**, and **pathways for employers and workers** — i.e. it ends in an actionable job-redesign
and training plan, not a trend report.

**Measurable results.** **19 JTMs launched, covering about 1.7 million employed residents** as at
Nov 2025. (Note: there is **no energy/power-sector JTM**; the nearest analogue is the separate
Energy & Chemicals Industry Transformation Map, with a skills framework built by SSG/EDB/WSG.)

- Source: MOM, *Written answer to PQ on update on Jobs Transformation Maps*, https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-questions-and-replies/2025/1104-written-answer-to-pq-on-update-on-jobs-transformation-maps (4 Nov 2025)
- Source: WSG JTM page, https://wsg.gov.sg/for-employers/jobs-transformation-maps.html

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. A **"Role Transformation Map" product inside Kafa'a**: for each of ENEC's top ~30 roles, a
   published page showing the technology drivers (AI diagnostics, digital twin, remote/robotic
   inspection, SMR design differences, advanced I&C), the tasks added/removed, the *redesigned*
   role profile, and the named reskilling pathway with dates. Publishing these is a trust act —
   people reskill when they can see the destination.
2. A **transformation timeline tied to real ENEC milestones** (KHNP support wind-down ~2030,
   ADVANCE/SMR gates, outage cycles, licence renewals) so the maps have deadlines rather than
   being perpetual "future of work" slides.

## 2. AUSTRALIA — Jobs and Skills Australia (owner: JSA, Australian Government)

### 2.1 Australian Skills Classification (ASC) — and its retirement (2021–2025)

**What it is / was.** A national open skills taxonomy splitting every occupation into **core
competencies, specialist tasks and technology tools**, published as open data.

**What actually happened — the most useful lesson here is a negative one.** JSA **stopped updating
the ASC in December 2023 and removed it from its website in January 2025**, citing identified
limitations including **data timeliness and the need for external validation**. Access now only via
data-access agreement for research (nationalskillstaxonomy@jobsandskills.gov.au).

- Source: JSA, *Australian Skills Classification*, https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/australian-skills-classification (updated 2025)

### 2.2 National Skills Taxonomy (NST) — in development (discussion paper 27 June 2024; pilot from 2025)

**Mechanism.** The NST will replace the ASC. The pilot tests **deriving skills from existing
datasets — notably VET units of competency** — rather than authoring a taxonomy from scratch. It
is governed by an advisory group spanning unions, employers, education providers and state
governments, chaired by a JSA Deputy Commissioner. Consultation ran under the banner *"Building a
system that puts people and skills first."* No final release date published as at Oct 2026.

- Source: JSA discussion paper (PDF), https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-06/national_skills_taxonomy_discussion_paper.pdf (27 Jun 2024)
- Source: JSA consultation, https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/consultations/have-your-say-building-system-puts-people-and-skills-first
- Source: JSA Commissioners update (PDF), https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-09/document_1_-_9._jsa_commissioners_update.pdf (Sep 2025)
- Source: NST pilot presentation, https://zenodo.org/records/18240102 (22 Oct 2025)

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. **Taxonomy maintenance as a funded, visible product feature, not a one-off project.** Give
   every competency in Kafa'a a `last_reviewed`, `reviewed_by`, `evidence_source` and a
   **staleness indicator**, with an admin "decay dashboard" listing competencies past review date.
   Australia killed a national taxonomy over timeliness — a plant competency framework that goes
   stale is a safety and licensing problem, not just a UX one.
2. **Derive competencies from artefacts you already maintain** (job task analyses, SAT/ADDIE
   training analyses, procedure libraries, licence requirement matrices, work-order histories)
   rather than hand-authoring a parallel list that immediately diverges from reality.
3. **Tripartite governance for the framework**: an ENEC competency council with operations,
   engineering, training, HR, union/employee reps and a FANR-literate member, chaired at
   executive level, with published minutes — so changes to competency standards are legitimate.

## 3. UNITED KINGDOM — Skills England, LSIPs, Lifelong Learning Entitlement

### 3.1 Skills England — *Assessment of priority skills to 2030* (published 12 Aug 2025, updated 5 Nov 2025)

**What it is.** The first UK quantitative, cross-sector assessment of future employment demand in
*priority occupations* across the 10 Industrial Strategy sectors, plus the education pathways that
supply them. Skills England was created to be "the single authoritative voice on the country's
current and future skills needs."

**Mechanism.** Uses SOC occupation codes for cross-sector comparability; for each priority
occupation it models 2025→2030 demand and maps the qualification level and education route that
feeds it. Skills England is explicit about the method's limits: SOC codes "do not work for all
types of employment, particularly in highly specialised and emerging roles" and "do not
specifically cover... critical thinking, and leadership."

**Measurable results (primary, read directly from the report).**
- Priority-occupation employment across the 10 sectors: **5.9M (2025) → 6.7M (2030), +0.9M, +15%** —
  **1.6× faster** than other employment in the same sectors (+0.8M, +9%, from 8.9M).
- **34%** of the additional demand needs level 2/3 qualifications; **66%** needs level 4+.
- **285,000 people enter priority occupations from the skills system each year**; **65%** of them
  at level 4+.
- Largest single occupations: **care workers/home carers +90,000** (one-ninth of all priority
  demand); **programmers/software development professionals +87,000**, listed as a priority by
  **seven of the ten sectors**.
- Biggest-growth sectors: **Digital Technologies, Adult Social Care, Construction, Engineering**.

**Companion product.** *Occupations in demand in 2025* (Skills England, Dec 2025) measures current
recruitment pressure. Skills England explicitly states the 2025 results **cannot be compared with
2024 because the methodology changed** — an unusually honest discontinuity disclosure.

- Source (primary, read directly): https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/assessment-of-priority-skills-to-2030/assessment-of-priority-skills-to-2030 (12 Aug 2025, updated 5 Nov 2025)
- Source: https://skillsengland.blog.gov.uk/2025/12/11/occupations-in-demand-in-2025 (11 Dec 2025)
- Source: Skills England annual skills report + sectoral needs assessments 2026, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/skills-england-annual-skills-report-and-sectoral-skills-needs-assessments-2026/sector-skills-needs-assessment-construction (2026)

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. **A 5-year ENEC demand model, published in-portal**: for each critical role, headcount today →
   headcount 2031, split by qualification level and by pipeline source (graduate programme,
   technician programme, internal progression, external hire, vendor). Attach **uncertainty
   bands** and state the assumptions — Skills England leads with "all estimates of future
   employment are highly uncertain", which is exactly the posture that keeps a forecast credible
   with engineers.
2. **Separate "pressure now" from "demand later"** into two distinct products (an Occupations in
   Demand board vs a 2030 Priority Skills view). Conflating them is how workforce dashboards lose
   trust.
3. **A methodology-change banner**: when the competency model or scoring changes, the UI says so
   and blocks naive year-on-year comparison. Cheap to build, disproportionately protective of
   analytical credibility.
4. **Name the gaps in your own taxonomy.** Skills England publicly states SOC misses emerging
   roles and soft skills. Kafa'a should carry an explicit "not yet modelled" list (e.g. SMR-specific
   roles, AI-assisted engineering) so users know what the absence of data means.

### 3.2 Lifelong Learning Entitlement — LLE (DfE, England; applications from Sept 2026, courses from Jan 2027)

**What it is.** A single credit-based post-18 funding entitlement replacing separate HE loans and
FE advanced learner loans.

**Mechanism — the key innovation is modularity.** Entitlement ≈ **four years of full-time study**
(~£38–39k at current fee caps **[figure varies by source — verify against GOV.UK before quoting]**),
expressed in **credits**: a full-time year = **120 credits**; learners can draw funding for a
**minimum of 30 credits and a maximum of 180 credits** in any course year/12-month period. Covers
**levels 4–6** and, for the first time, **individual modules** of HTQs (L4–5) and of priority-
aligned full L6 qualifications — not only whole qualifications. Available to age **60**; prior
study reduces remaining entitlement; repayment on Plan 5 terms (9% above £25,000).

- Source: GOV.UK policy paper, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/lifelong-learning-entitlement-lle-overview/lifelong-learning-entitlement-overview
- Source: IFS analysis, https://ifs.org.uk/articles/what-does-lifelong-learning-entitlement-mean-post-18-education

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. **A credit currency for ENEC learning.** Express every learning asset — course, simulator
   block, OJT sign-off, vendor training, conference, secondment — in a single credit unit, so
   effort is comparable and a partial journey still banks value. This is the mechanism that makes
   a competence wallet meaningful rather than a list of certificate images.
2. **Modular stacking toward a real qualification**: 30-credit modules that accumulate into a
   Khalifa University / NQF-recognised award, so an operator who completes four modules over
   three years has a portable qualification and not just internal credit. Pair with §6.3 (UAE NQF)
   so the credits map to NQF levels from day one.
3. **A visible remaining-entitlement balance** (credits and funded time) in the employee profile,
   including the deduction logic. Transparency of balance is what drove LLE's design.

### 3.3 Local Skills Improvement Plans — LSIPs (DfE/England, statutory since the Skills and Post-16 Education Act 2022)

**What it is.** Employer-led, place-based plans that make local training provision answer to local
employer demand; employer representative bodies (chambers) lead them, and colleges must have
regard to them.

**Status 2025–26.** 2025 progress reports were published; core LSIP funding was due to end March
2025 and was contested; Government remains committed to the model but with a greater role for
Mayors and Strategic Authorities, suggesting consolidation into mayoral/regional strategies.

- Source: Business West, *Local Skills Improvement Plans 2025 Progress Reports Released*, https://www.businesswest.co.uk/blog/blog-posts/local-skills-improvement-plans-2025-progress-reports-released/ (2025)
- Source: Learning and Work Institute, https://learningandwork.org.uk/towards-a-collaborative-approach-to-building-a-skilled-workforce-for-tomorrow/ (2025)
- Source: Newcastle University Policy, https://policy.ncl.ac.uk/news/the-evolution-of-regional-education-partnerships (2025)

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. A **"Regional Skills Plan — Al Dhafra / Barakah"** module: ENEC publishes its forward
   competency demand to local education providers (ADVETI/ATHS, Khalifa University, Al Dhafra
   institutes), and those providers respond in-portal with planned cohort sizes and course
   changes. The feature is the *commitment loop*: demand signal in, provision commitment back,
   both visible and tracked against delivery.
2. **A supply-chain/contractor skills view**: Barakah's competence risk sits substantially with
   contractors and vendors. LSIP's lesson is that the plan must cover the whole local labour
   market, not just the anchor employer's own payroll.

## 4. CANADA — Future Skills Centre (owner: FSC, funded by ESDC, Government of Canada)

**What it is.** A national skills-innovation body (est. Dec 2018) that funds, evaluates and scales
pilot projects, rather than delivering training itself. Hosted by Toronto Metropolitan University
with the Conference Board of Canada and Blueprint.

**Mechanism — the distinctive design is "pilot + matched evaluator + publish".** Every innovation
pilot is matched with an independent evaluator to support monitoring and learning and surface
actionable insights (new evaluation partners announced 18 Aug 2025). Findings are published
openly so other employers/regions can adopt them.

**Measurable results (primary, read directly from the 2025 Impact Report PDF, published 12 Sep 2025):**
- **$309M invested** in research and pilot projects; **400** skills-innovation pilot and research
  projects; **3,100+ project partners**.
- **1.14M workers and employers** accessed FSC resources supporting labour-market pathways.
- **105,500+ people received hands-on training or new job opportunities.**
- **78% of completed projects report community adoption, expansion or further investment** — the
  single best "did it stick?" metric found in this whole review.
- **71%** of projects support underserved groups; **23%** of initiatives led by organisations from
  underrepresented groups; **32%** of projects led by employers or industry associations.
- **870+ research and insights reports**, with **half of users saying they applied the resource in
  their own work**; **9 regional partnerships**; **1,100+ policymakers engaged**.
- Federal evaluation (2018–2023) separately records **~$288.7M in contributions**, **230+
  projects**, **70%+ targeting under-represented groups**.

- Source (PDF, primary, verified by direct download): FSC, *Building a Resilient Workforce: Six Years of Impact — Impact Report 2025*, https://fsc-ccf.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/FSC-Impact-Report-EN-FINAL.pdf (12 Sep 2025)
- Source: ESDC, *Evaluation of the Future Skills program – 2018 to 2023*, https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/corporate/reports/evaluations/future-skills-2018-to-2023.html

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. **A "Skills Pilots" module**: ENEC runs 6–10 workforce experiments a year (e.g. VR outage
   training, AI-assisted procedure authoring, accelerated SRO pipeline, female technician
   pathway), each registered in Kafa'a with a hypothesis, a comparison group, a named evaluator
   and a published result — including failures. This converts L&D from spend to evidence.
2. **Adopt "% of pilots adopted/expanded/further funded" as a headline KPI** on the executive
   dashboard, alongside completion rates. 78% is a benchmark worth stating out loud.
3. **An open insights library** with usage-and-application tracking ("did you apply this?"), so
   ENEC can show the regulator and the board that learning products change practice.

## 5. GERMANY, FINLAND, KOREA — three more mechanisms worth copying

### 5.1 Germany — Nationale Weiterbildungsstrategie (NWS), Qualifizierungsgeld, Bildungszeit
(owner: BMAS + BMBF + social partners; NWS launched 2019, renewed 27 Sep 2022; reform proposals 2025)

**Mechanism.** Beyond the famous dual system (employer-led apprenticeship + vocational school with
chamber-set standards), the NWS adds two instruments aimed squarely at *incumbent* workers in
structurally changing firms:
- **Qualifizierungsgeld** — a wage-replacement benefit funded by the Federal Employment Agency for
  employees of firms undergoing structural transformation, so staff can retrain *without leaving
  the company and without the company carrying the full wage cost*.
- **Bildungszeit / Bildungsteilzeit** — statutory educational leave and part-time educational
  leave, letting employees direct their own further training.
2025 reform proposals include cutting the **minimum course length from 120 to 60 hours** and
simplifying provider certification — i.e. making funded training shorter and more modular.

Caveat: these results are from policy announcements, draft legislation (BMAS Referentenentwurf,
Bundestag Drucksache 20/6518) and a July 2025 lobby-register entry. **No participation outcome
data was located** — treat enactment status as **[UNVERIFIED]**.

- Source: BMBF/NWS launch coverage, https://www.deutsche-handwerks-zeitung.de/?p=260493 (Sep 2022)
- Source: Haufe, *Geförderte Bildungszeit: Heil kündigt Gesetzentwurf an*, https://www.haufe.de/personal/neues-lernen/gefoerderte-bildungszeit-heil-kuendigt-gesetzentwurf-an_589614_576154.html
- Source: BMAS draft law (PDF), https://cdn.table.media/assets/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Referentenentwurf_BMAS_Gesetz-zur-Staerkung-der-Aus-und-Weiterbildungsfoerderung-und-Einfuehrung-einer-Bildungszeit.pdf (Dec 2022)
- Source: Bundestag lobby register, reform of NWS/Qualifizierungsgeld, https://www.lobbyregister.bundestag.de/inhalte-der-interessenvertretung/regelungsvorhabensuche/RV0018430 (21 Jul 2025)
- Source: Eurydice Germany VET reforms, https://eurydice.eacea.ec.europa.eu/de/national-education-systems/germany/reformen-im-bereich-der-berufsausbildung-und-der

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. A **"transition retainer"** pattern for the 2030 handover: when a role is slated to change
   (KHNP support ending, SMR conversion, automation of a task set), the employee enters a funded
   retraining status with protected salary and a defined return role — modelled, tracked and
   *visible in the portal* so it reads as investment rather than redundancy risk.
2. **60-hour modular minimum** rather than multi-week block courses, so shift-working plant staff
   can actually complete training between outage cycles.
3. **Dual-system structure for the ENEC technician pipeline**: alternating plant placement and
   institute study, with chamber-equivalent external standard-setting (FANR/NQC/Khalifa) so the
   qualification is recognised outside ENEC.

### 5.2 Finland — Continuous Learning reform + JOTPA (Service Centre for Continuous Learning and Employment)
(owner: Ministry of Education and Culture + Ministry of Economic Affairs and Employment; Act in force Sept 2021; EU-RRF reform elements due complete 30 Jun 2025)

**Mechanism.** JOTPA's mandate is deliberately narrow and useful: **analyse labour-market skills
needs, finance training that the existing system does not supply, develop guidance services, and
support regional cooperation** — it buys and brokers provision rather than owning institutions.
Its four reform elements were: the service centre itself, a **labour and competence needs
forecasting system**, digital/green-transition training, and **tailored guidance**.

**Results.** In 2022 JOTPA ran **six government grant programmes financing 170+ projects**, plus
procurements, an outreach pilot and a regional pilot, with roughly **17,000 people expected** in
financed services (planned, not confirmed outturn). A 2025 pilot targets **~1,900 students** in
nursing, social work, early childhood and special education. **Adult education support
(aikuiskoulutustuki) was abolished from August 2024** to cut public expenditure — a reminder that
individual learning subsidies are politically fragile.

- Source: European Commission, *Reform: continuous learning*, https://commission.europa.eu/projects/reform-continuous-learning_en
- Source: Eurydice Finland, national reforms in adult education and training, https://eurydice.eacea.ec.europa.eu/national-education-systems/finland/national-reforms-related-adult-education-and-training
- Source: Cedefop, https://www.cedefop.europa.eu/en/node/153112

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. **A "gap-filling procurement" function**: Kafa'a identifies competencies with *no* adequate
   provider in the UAE market, and that list becomes a formal procurement/partnership backlog with
   owners and dates. Most L&D portals only match learners to existing supply; the valuable move is
   naming the supply that doesn't exist yet (very relevant for SMR-specific competencies).
2. **Outreach to non-participants as a tracked programme.** Finland funds an explicit outreach
   pilot for people who don't come forward. Kafa'a should report *who is not using it* (by shift,
   site, grade, nationality, gender) and treat that as the primary equity metric.

### 5.3 Korea — National Competency Standards (NCS) + K-Digital Training
(owner: Ministry of Employment and Labor + HRD Korea + KRIVET; NCS development from 2002; K-Digital from 2021; innovation plan from 2024)

**Mechanism.**
- **NCS** codifies occupations into **competency units → competency elements**, each with
  performance criteria and level, and then links each unit to an **NCS learning module** and to
  **qualifications** — so training content, assessment and certification all hang off one spine.
  Since 2014 Korea has run **course-based qualifications** (at industrial-technician level), where
  completing an NCS-aligned course *is* the route to the qualification rather than only a
  separate exam.
- **K-Digital Training** funds high-quality advanced/digital training through the **National
  Tomorrow Learning Card**, a government-funded lifelong learning voucher covering full training
  expenses. The **2024 innovation plan** widened scope from digital-only to advanced and
  digital-convergence technologies after noting advanced-field courses were only **~6% of the
  total (29 of 470)**; a new call added 21 courses from 18 institutions (50 courses / 40
  institutions in that tranche).

Honest caveat from the research literature: NCS has been criticised because it "is actually not
used to redesign... qualifications such as establishment of new qualification types", and
university adoption of NCS-based courses has lagged — a standard can exist on paper without
changing practice.

- Source: MOEL, https://moel.go.kr/english/news/moelNewsDetail.do?idx=3137
- Source: 2024 K-Digital innovation plan coverage (Korean), https://newsseoul.co.kr/news/view/1065581415705245 (2024)
- Source: KRIVET/academic analysis of NCS–qualification linkage, https://papers.iafor.org/wp-content/uploads/papers/aceid2017/ACEID2017_33065.pdf (2017)
- Source: Cedefop Korea VET note, https://www.cedefop.europa.eu/en/print/pdf/node/152904
- NCS portal: https://www.ncs.go.kr **[level structure not verified against the portal — confirm before quoting]**

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. **Course-based qualification**: make completing an ENEC-accredited competency programme
   (assessed on the job, not just by exam) a direct route to a recognised credential, with the
   assessment evidence captured in Kafa'a. This is the highest-value borrow for the KHNP handover,
   where what matters is demonstrated competence, not seat time.
2. **One spine, three uses**: the same competency unit record drives (a) the learning module,
   (b) the assessment instrument, and (c) the qualification claim. Kafa'a should enforce this as a
   data constraint — no learning asset without a linked competency unit, no assessment without a
   linked criterion.
3. **Learn from the failure mode**: audit annually what % of competency units actually have a live
   learning module *and* a live assessment *and* a current qualification linkage. Report the
   orphan rate. A standard with no delivery attached is Korea's documented weakness.

### 6.1 Jahiz — the federal future-skills platform (owner: Government Development and the Future Office + FAHR; launched November 2022)

**This is the closest existing UAE product to Kafa'a, and the one Kafa'a will be compared against.**

performance management system**. Later extensions: a FAHR partnership with **Udacity** (April

Note: no public participation or completion figures were found — **[UNVERIFIED]** on outcomes.

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. **A "Future Skills Wallet" for ENEC, explicitly wired to performance management** — this is
   Jahiz's sharpest design decision. Skills earned must appear in the appraisal and promotion
   record, or the portal becomes optional. Kafa'a should write the linkage both ways: the wallet
   feeds the appraisal, and the appraisal's development goals create wallet targets.
2. **Mirror the four-group structure so ENEC staff recognise it**, then add the nuclear fifth:
   Digital / 10X / Data & AI / New Economy (incl. net-zero, which is ENEC's actual product) /
   **Nuclear Safety & Operational Excellence**. National-framework alignment is nearly free
   reputationally and makes Kafa'a legible to federal stakeholders.
3. **A declared, time-boxed national-style target** ("every ENEC employee holds the 5 core future

### 6.2 MoHRE skill-based work permits / UAE Occupational Classification (owner: Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation)

**Mechanism.** The private-sector classification holds **725 occupational categories, in nine
occupational levels, grouped into five skill levels**, ISCO-aligned. Each job title carries a code
and short description; the skill level determines whether a degree certificate must be supplied
with a work-permit application. A worker is classified **skilled** where they hold a qualification
above high school, **accredited by the relevant authorities**, *and* earn at least **AED 4,000/month
excluding commissions**.

**Measurable results.** UAE workforce grew **12.4% in 2025**; Q1 2026 private-sector workforce grew
**2.5%** with rising skilled-job share (MoHRE figures via Gulf News / People Matters).

- Source: Gulf News, *UAE workforce jumps 12.4% in 2025 as new firms surge, MoHRE says*, https://gulfnews.com/uae/government/uae-workforce-jumps-124-in-2025-as-new-firms-surge-mohre-says-1.500416665 (2026)

- Source: People Matters ME, Q1 2026 workforce data, https://me.peoplemattersglobal.com/news/economy-policy/uae-private-sector-workforce-grows-25percent-in-q1-2026-as-demand-for-skilled-talent-rises-50410 (2026)

**What Kafa'a could borrow.**
1. **Map every ENEC role to its MoHRE occupational code and skill level** as a first-class field.
   This makes Kafa'a's data interoperable with national reporting, Emiratisation reporting and
   work-permit processes in one stroke, and lets ENEC report its skilled-share against the
   national trend line.
2. **A "skill-level uplift" metric**: track movement of the ENEC + contractor workforce up the
   five national skill levels over time. It is an externally defined, non-gameable progress
   measure that a board and a ministry both already understand.

### 6.3 National Qualifications Centre (NQC) and the UAE National Qualifications Framework
(owner: NQC, Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research; NQF approved by Cabinet Resolution 2024; earlier NQA framework of nine levels)

**Mechanism.** The NQF spans general education, higher education and TVET; **each level defines the
knowledge, skills and competencies required** for a qualification. The same Cabinet Resolution
defines **Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL)** as "an assessment process aimed at evaluating the
knowledge and skills that an individual has acquired through prior learning and experience."
Foreign vocational qualifications are recognised through the national authority. CAA + NQC held a
2023 seminar encouraging expansion of **RPL and micro-credentials** after quality assurance.

**Live development (July 2026).** NQC ran a workshop on building a **national framework for
recognising work experience** — a system that lets people convert workplace experience into
**academic hours or vocational qualifications**. Higher-education institutions, TVET providers and
employers attended, and **private-sector firms expressed interest in piloting the initiative**.

- Source: WAM, *National Qualifications Centre discusses development of national framework for recognition of work experience*, https://www.wam.ae/en/article/c12hbfm-national-qualifications-centre-discusses (Jul 2026)
- Source: UAE Cabinet Resolution on the National Qualifications Framework, https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/2615/download (2024)
- Source: CAA, *Recognition of Prior Learning and Micro-credentials*, https://caa.ae/MediaCenter/Pages/NEWS/Recognition-of-Prior-Learning-and-Micro-.aspx (2023)
- Source: ADEK, *National Qualification Framework in the Emirates*, https://adek.gov.ae/Education-System/Higher-Education/National-Qualification-Framework-in-the-Emirates

**What Kafa'a could borrow — this is the highest-leverage UAE alignment available.**
1. **Tag every Kafa'a competency and learning asset with an NQF level.** Then a Kafa'a profile is
   automatically readable as a qualifications claim, and internal progression maps onto a national
   ladder rather than an ENEC-only grade scheme.
2. **Build an RPL engine**: structured evidence capture (OJT sign-offs, simulator records,
   authorisations held, years at duty, supervisor attestation, assessment results) packaged into
   an RPL submission that NQC-recognised assessors can evaluate. For ENEC this converts a decade
   of Barakah operating experience — much of it currently undocumented outside internal systems —
   into recognised qualifications. It is also the cleanest answer to "what happens to our people
   when KHNP support ends": their accumulated competence becomes formally certified.
3. **Volunteer ENEC as the pilot employer** for NQC's work-experience recognition framework. The
   July 2026 workshop explicitly sought private-sector pilot partners; a nuclear operator with
   rigorous, auditable competence records is close to an ideal pilot, and it would give Kafa'a
   national-framework status rather than vendor status.

   learner must assemble. For nuclear, the applied leg (simulator, OJT, shadow authorisation) is
   mandatory anyway, so this matches reality.
2. **Sector-partnership agreements as a portal object**: ENEC + a ministry/regulator + a provider
   sign a competency-delivery agreement with named cohorts and dates, tracked in Kafa'a. Saudi's
   trilateral pattern is a good template for ENEC–FANR–Khalifa University or ENEC–ADNOC/TAQA
   cross-sector talent flows.
3. **A declared national-scale number** with a public counter. Both Saudi and Jahiz show that a
   single memorable target ("300,000", "20 skills in one year") drives adoption more than a
   well-specified roadmap.

## 8. CROSS-CUTTING SYNTHESIS — what the benchmarks agree on

1. **Taxonomy → telemetry → record → recommendation → entitlement → redesign.** Every mature system
   has all six layers. Portals that stop at "catalogue + completion tracking" don't change
   behaviour. Singapore is the only one with the full stack and is therefore the primary model.
2. **The task layer is where the future-skills action is.** Singapore's 37,000-task analysis and
   its 24% AI-exposure finding are only possible because roles decompose into tasks. Australia's
   NST pilot is doing the same thing from VET units. Kafa'a needs tasks between role and
   competency or it cannot answer the questions it will be asked in 2028.
3. **Verified provenance is the product.** CSP, Jahiz's wallet and the UAE NQF all converge on
   "who attested this, and can a third party trust it." Self-reported skills have near-zero value.
4. **Sharing is rarer than viewing.** CSP: 315,000 accessed, 4,900 shared. Design for the handful
   of high-stakes moments (applying for a role, an authorisation review, a handover) rather than
   assuming daily engagement.
5. **Entitlements need time, not only money.** Singapore added a training allowance a year after
   the credit; Germany's whole NWS instrument set is about wage replacement and educational leave.
   A credit without protected hours produces enrolments and not completions.
6. **Taxonomies rot.** Australia retired a national classification over timeliness. Korea's NCS is
   criticised for standards without delivery. Build maintenance, staleness flags and an orphan-rate
   audit in from the start.
7. **Publish the method and its limits.** Skills England states uncertainty, SOC's blind spots, and
   a methodology break that voids year-on-year comparison. In a nuclear organisation, that posture
   is the difference between a dashboard engineers use and one they ignore.
8. **Evaluate and publish, including failures.** Canada's FSC is the benchmark: every pilot has a
   matched evaluator and 78% adoption is a reportable KPI.
9. **Align to the national frame, because it's free credibility.** Jahiz's four skill groups, the
   NQF levels, MoHRE occupational codes and Nafis reporting are all available hooks. Tagging to
   them costs little and makes Kafa'a legible to FANR, MoHRE, NQC and the board at once.
