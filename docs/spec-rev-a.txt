# Kafa'a Nuclear Skills Platform
كفاءة: منصة المهارات النووية
A product roadmap and specification for a careers, competency, qualification, training and partner-ecosystem platform for the Emirates Nuclear Energy Company and the UAE nuclear sector. It reimagines the UAE banking skills demo at uaecb.netlify.app for a licensed, safety-critical industry. "Kafa'a" (Arabic for competence or qualification) is a working name.

Section 1
## Executive summary
What it is. Kafa'a is a bilingual (Arabic and English) web platform that does for nuclear energy what the uaecb demo tries to do for UAE banking. It publishes a map of nuclear careers and the competencies each role needs. It holds each employee's qualification status, training history and assessment results. It tracks operator licensing milestones under FANR-REG-17, runs the talent pipeline from school outreach to scholarships, and gives suppliers, universities and international partners a structured way in.
Who it serves. The public and students; applicants to ENEC's talent programmes; staff across ENEC, ENEC Operations, ENEC Commercial and ENEC Consulting; licensed operators and their instructors; managers and HR; suppliers and contractors; universities and research partners. Regulators appear only as observers of aggregate reports that ENEC chooses to share. They never use Kafa'a as a regulatory system.
Why ENEC. ENEC runs one of the most training-intensive workforces in the region, with a regulated licensing regime, a stated Emiratization agenda, a large local supply chain and an advanced-reactor programme that will need new skills. Its Enterprise Strategy 2025-2029 names "People & Culture" as one of three themes (enec.ae).

### Five commitments that separate Kafa'a from its source
Real, sourced data or a visible "sample" label. uaecb shows hard-coded marketing numbers (155+ competencies against 16 in the code). Kafa'a shows no number without a source or a SAMPLE tag.
Access control enforced on the server. uaecb's middleware is disabled and every HR page is public. Kafa'a checks every request against role and scope in the database.
No named staff on public pages, and no security-sensitive plant information anywhere. uaecb embeds about 1,500 real bank employees in org charts. Kafa'a shows entities and roles, never people.
A tracker, not an authority. FANR certifies operators; ENEC's training organisation qualifies staff. Kafa'a mirrors status from those systems of record and says so on every screen.
Qualification is not a game. Points, streaks and leaderboards are removed from qualification and licensing work. Recognition stays for outreach and students.
### Scope at a glance
Sixteen modules in four zones: a public site, a staff portal, a partner portal and an administration console (Section 5). Delivery runs from discovery through an MVP public site to staff, partner and future-workforce phases (Section 13). Kafa'a complements Armie's existing ENEC NEXUS concept, which governs AI agents across ENEC's enterprise. Kafa'a is the people and skills layer; NEXUS is the agent operating layer (Section 12).
Section 2
## What we learned from uaecb
uaecb.netlify.app is the "UAE Central Bank Banking Skills Platform, an ADGM Academy Partnership", deployed from github.com/armieahmed7/uae-banking-platform. It is modelled on Singapore's IBF and built with Next.js 15, React 19, Tailwind with shadcn/ui, Prisma 6 on Postgres, NextAuth and OpenAI gpt-3.5-turbo. It has 74 page routes and 29 API routes. The analysis behind this section crawled all 74 routes on 8 Oct 2026 and read the source at commit 784c1f7.
### Structure in brief
 | Area | What uaecb offers | Real or mock
 | Public portal | IBF-style landing with three audience cards (Professionals, Institutions, Job Seekers), mega-menu, About, Partnership, Mission, 20 FAQs, Contact, Demo request | Static
 | Career architecture | 38 retail-banking roles in 5 departments; hierarchy, department and career-path views; market outlook, Emiratization and "Arabic required" flags; career simulator; salary guide | Constants in a 2,314-line file; about 160 referenced role ids, most undefined
 | Competency framework | 4 categories x 4 competencies = 16 coded competencies (TB, DT, LM, SB), each on 4 levels from Foundation to Expert | UI constants; claims 155
 | Assessments | 6 MCQ assessments of 5 questions each, quick assessment, video and AI video interviews, certificate generation | Partly database; logged-out page hangs on "Loading"
 | Learning | 5 courses, 3 learning paths, 2 certifications, "blockchain" certificates, AI podcasts, VR modules, challenges | Mock; no Course or Enrolment model
 | Jobs and resume | Job board with match score, resume builder with 4 templates, ATS optimiser | Job API returns an error; 0 jobs live
 | Gamification | Points, levels, streaks, badges, global leaderboards | Schema exists; UI mock

 | Arabic | Dictionaries, RTL flag, IBM Plex Sans Arabic, Arabic role titles | Only 4 pages translated
### Three screens, described
Career pathways
A grid of department tiles above a searchable list of role cards. Each card shows level, years of experience, a salary range, an outlook chip (At Risk, Stable, Growing), an Emiratization badge and an "Arabic Required" flag. A side panel opens the role, and an "Ask Ali" button offers AI advice. This is the strongest pattern in the product and the direct ancestor of the Nuclear Career Map.
Enterprise dashboard
Six KPI tiles (1,245 employees, 74% average skill, 45% Emiratization) over tabs for performance, skills gaps, learning and workforce planning. The layout is sound. The figures are invented, contradict other pages (2,341 employees elsewhere) and are visible to anyone without signing in.
Bank organization charts
A bank picker and a zoomable tree of named executives with photos, emails and LinkedIn links, scraped from public sources. It looks impressive in a demo and is the clearest privacy anti-pattern in the codebase. Kafa'a replaces it with an entity and role structure that names no one.
### Keep
The three-audience front door, translated to nuclear audiences.
The role data shape: title in two languages, level, responsibilities, required competencies, entry routes, vertical, lateral and cross-functional progressions, Emiratization and Arabic flags.
Competency x proficiency-level framework, linked to roles.
Assessment structure (question bank, timed attempt, per-competency scoring) and the UserCertification status lifecycle (planned, in progress, completed, expired, renewed), which maps neatly onto qualification expiry.
Individual development plans with activities and milestones.
Consent capture and an audit log on sign-in.
A contextual AI advisor, rebuilt with governance.
IBM Plex Sans Arabic and mirrored layouts for RTL.
### Fix
 | uaecb weakness | Evidence | Kafa'a rule
 | No working access control | middleware.ts matcher is empty; userType is never checked; HR, admin and regulator pages are public | Deny by default. Server-side checks on every page and API, plus row-level security in the database. Automated tests per role.
 | Mock data presented as fact | About 60 of 74 pages use static constants; headline numbers contradict each other | Every figure carries a source or a SAMPLE label. Demo data lives in a separate, clearly marked tenant.
 | Privacy | About 1,500 real bank employees in org charts; Emirates ID and date of birth collected without a stated purpose | No named staff on public pages. Collect identity data only through UAE Pass where needed, with purpose limitation.
 | Exposed debug endpoints | /api/debug/env and /api/ai/diagnostic leak the OpenAI key prefix and length; /api/auth/debug and /api/test-email are public | No debug routes in production builds; CI fails if any route matches a debug pattern.
 | Broken pages | /jobs API error, /auth/error crash, hydration errors on 4 pages, 5 dead admin links, 6 stubs | A route ships only with content, an empty state and an error state. A link checker runs in CI.
 | Broken demo access | All documented demo logins return 401 | Demo personas live in a demo tenant with working, rotated credentials and no production data.
 | Open AI cost sink | Unauthenticated chat calls OpenAI directly | Rate-limited, grounded advisor with a model gateway, logging and evaluation (M13).
 | Shallow Arabic | 4 pages translated; the toggle flips direction but not content | Arabic parity is a release gate for every route (M15).
 | Weak secrets handling | NEXTAUTH_SECRET placeholder in netlify.toml; 2FA secret stored in plain text; email verification disabled | Secrets in a managed vault, encrypted at rest; MFA through UAE Pass or an authenticator; verification enforced.

### Barakah Nuclear Energy Plant
Four APR-1400 pressurised water reactors in the Al Dhafra Region of Abu Dhabi, each rated up to 1,400 MWe gross with a design life of at least 60 years (enec.ae). Together they produce about 40 TWh a year, which ENEC says covers 25% of UAE electricity demand and avoids 22.4 Mt of CO2 a year (enec.ae). The IAEA gives a 21.8% nuclear share of UAE generation in 2024 (NucNet).

### Partners and supply chain, in one paragraph
KEPCO led the prime contract (about USD 20bn, Dec 2009) with KHNP, KEPCO E&C, KEPCO Nuclear Fuel, KEPCO KPS, Doosan, Hyundai E&C, Samsung C&T and Westinghouse. KHNP and EDF support operations and maintenance. Fuel comes from a set of front-end suppliers (URENCO, ConverDyn, Rio Tinto, Uranium One, Orano, Tenex), Kazatomprom since Dec 2023, and Framatome assemblies from Jul 2025. The ADVANCE programme, launched 30 Nov 2023, evaluates SMRs and microreactors and produced MoUs with GE Hitachi, TerraPower, Westinghouse, X-energy, USNC, MoltexFLEX and CNNC, followed by Newcleo, GE Vernova Hitachi, Samsung C&T and others. More than 2,000 UAE companies hold contracts, and ENEC applies the national In-Country Value programme in tenders. Section 9 gives the full seed list.
Two items often repeated in commentary are not verified and are excluded from this spec: an ENEC-Microsoft AI partnership, and an "Energy Alliance" with ADNOC, Masdar and EWEC under that name.

## Audiences and personas
uaecb had five user types and enforced none of them. Kafa'a has fourteen personas in four access tiers. Personas are archetypes: none is a real person and none carries a real name.
PUBLIC · no sign-in
Curious resident or visitor
Goals: understand what Barakah does, who works there and how safety and emergency planning work.
Uses: Home, About, Community & ESG, Safety culture explainer, Dalil (public mode).
PUBLIC → APPLICANT
School student or parent
Goals: learn which subjects lead to nuclear careers; find Mustaqbali, DNT and scholarship routes.
Uses: Career Map (student view), Pathways, booklets, outreach events.
APPLICANT · light account
University student or graduate
Goals: compare Energy Pioneers scholarships, GDP and GPO; apply for internships; see what an SRO career involves.
Uses: Pathways, programme pages, application tracker, Dalil.
APPLICANT · light account
Experienced job seeker
Goals: see if their fossil, naval or overseas nuclear experience counts towards an RO or SRO route; find open roles.
Uses: Career Map with REG-17 entry routes, eligibility explainer, link out to ENEC careers.
STAFF
Employee in a job family
Goals: see their qualification status and what expires next; plan the next role; book training.
Uses: My dashboard, My qualifications, My learning, Development plan.
STAFF · licensed
Licensed operator (RO or SRO)
Goals: never lose certificate validity; see quarterly shift count, medical due date, requalification window.
Uses: Licence tracker, continuing-training schedule, simulator bookings.
STAFF · elevated
Instructor or training coordinator
Goals: build SAT-based courses, schedule simulator time, record JPM and task evaluations, keep qualification cards current.
Uses: Training admin, JPM evaluator, item bank, cohort views.
STAFF · elevated
Line manager or shift supervisor
Goals: know the team is qualified for scheduled work; spot expiry risks; approve development plans.
Uses: Team qualification matrix, expiry calendar, proficiency status.
STAFF · elevated
Licensing coordinator
Goals: prepare candidate lists, track exam milestones and certificate validity, raise lapse notices in time.
Uses: Licensing tracker (programme view), reporting calendar.
STAFF · elevated
HR and Emiratization analyst
Goals: monitor pipeline health, Emiratization by job family and level, critical-role succession depth.
Uses: Workforce analytics, Emiratization dashboard, pipeline funnel.
PARTNER
Supplier or contractor
Goals: learn how to become a Barakah supplier, complete supplier courses, prove contractor staff qualifications, improve ICV.
Uses: Partner onboarding, Supplier academy, Contractor equivalency, ICV guide.
PARTNER
University or training partner
Goals: align curricula to competency needs, host interns, track scholarship cohorts, propose research.
Uses: University workspace, competency library, research portal.
PARTNER
International partner
Goals: understand ENEC's capability-building model; coordinate exchanges (KHNP, EDF, NPCIL-style O&M cooperation).
Uses: Partner directory, exchange programme pages, ENEC Consulting link.
OBSERVER · read-only
Regulator or government observer
Goals: view aggregate workforce and training indicators that ENEC chooses to publish to them.
Uses: Observer reports only. No individual records. Not a channel for regulatory submissions.

Section 6
## Module specifications
Sixteen modules. Each lists purpose, users, key screens, features, data, business rules and acceptance criteria. Module IDs (M01 to M16) are used in the roadmap and the mapping table.
M01
### Nuclear Career MapPublicStaff
Purpose
An explorable catalogue of nuclear job families and roles, with real responsibilities, qualifications, training paths, licensing authority and progression rules. It is the public face of the platform and the reference every other module links to.
Users
Students, parents, applicants, staff planning a move, HR, universities aligning curricula.
Key screens
/careers: job family tiles (Operations, Maintenance, Engineering, Radiation Protection, Chemistry, Nuclear Fuel, Outage & Work Management, Emergency Preparedness, Security, Cyber & IT/OT, Safeguards, Quality, Nuclear Oversight, Licensing, Nuclear Training, Health & Safety, Environment, Supply Chain, Fire Protection, Corporate), search, filters for entry level, regulated role and education.
/careers/roles/:slug: what the role does, qualifications, training path with durations, licensing or authorising body, entry routes, next roles, related competencies, "Ask Dalil about this role".
/careers/paths: the three uaecb views (hierarchy, family, path) plus an operator ladder view.
Features
A "regulated role" badge on RO, SRO and roles with regulated requirements, linking to the regulation.
A basis badge on each role: Barakah-specific when an ENEC or FANR source describes it, Generic reference when it follows IAEA SSG-75, Uncertain when the Barakah structure is unconfirmed (for example the Shift Technical Advisor).
Progression edges carry conditions (months of experience, programmes, exams) as structured data, not prose.
Emiratization flags only where ENEC publicly reserves a role, for example Administrative Assistant vacancies open only to UAE nationals (enec.ae).
Salary bands and "automation risk" from uaecb are removed. In their place, a link to M11 shows which skills advanced reactors may need.

M02
### Competency and Qualification FrameworkPublic libraryStaff
Purpose
Define what "competent" means for each role and record who is qualified to do which task. It follows the Systematic Approach to Training, which ENEC Operations adopted as described by INPO and which FANR requires (CNS report Art. 9; FANR-REG-16 Art. 9): job and task analysis produces task lists, task lists produce qualification cards, and qualification cards are completed through on-the-job training and task performance evaluations, including job performance measures (JPMs).
Users
Staff (own record), instructors and evaluators, managers (team view), framework owners, universities (public library only).
Key screens
/competencies: public library of domains and levels with linked roles.
/my/quals: my qualification cards, task by task, with evaluator, date and expiry.
/training/quals/:standard: qualification standard editor with versions.
/team/quals: matrix of people (in my scope) against qualifications, coloured by status.
Features
Two layers, kept separate. A competency layer for development (proficiency levels) and a qualification layer for authorisation (qualified or not, per task, with expiry). uaecb has only the first. Nuclear work needs the second.
Competency domains (replacing uaecb's TB/DT/LM/SB): plant fundamentals and APR-1400 systems; conduct of operations; maintenance practices; radiation protection; chemistry; engineering and design authority; nuclear safety and licensing basis; human performance; nuclear safety culture (the 10 traits); emergency preparedness; security awareness (general content only); quality and NQA-1; leadership; digital and AI.
Levels: Awareness, Practitioner (works under supervision), Qualified (works independently), Expert (can train and evaluate others). These mirror uaecb's four levels with nuclear meanings.
Qualification cards with task lists, linked JPMs, prerequisites and requalification interval.
Sign-off flow: trainee → qualified evaluator → supervisor acknowledgement.
Expiry alerts at configurable intervals, defaulting to 90, 60 and 30 days.
"Who is qualified for task X" lookup for work planners, limited to people in scope.
Change impact: when a task or procedure changes after a modification or an operating-experience lesson, affected qualifications are flagged for retraining.
Data
Competency, ProficiencyLevel, RoleCompetency, QualificationStandard (version, owner, interval), TaskItem, JPM, QualificationRecord, Evaluation, RequalRule.
Business rules
An evaluator cannot sign off their own qualification, and must hold the evaluator qualification for that standard.
An expired qualification cannot be set to current without a new evaluation record.
Records are append-only; corrections create a new entry with a reason.
Until ENEC approves Kafa'a as the system of record, the qualification layer mirrors ENEC's training records system and labels itself as a mirror.
Contractor staff follow the same standards through training or an equivalency decision (CNS report), handled in M09.
Acceptance
A qualification card shows each task's status, evaluator role, date and expiry in both languages.
An attempt to self-sign returns a clear error and writes an audit event.
Thirty days before expiry, the person and their manager receive a notice; the matrix shows the item as "due".
Revising a qualification standard creates a new version and lists every record that needs review.

Human hand-off: careers team, training coordinator or partner desk, with the conversation passed on only if the user agrees.
Concerns: stops and shows the ECP channels (see M08).
Operations: model gateway with a UAE-hosted model option, rate limits per user and per IP, logging with a retention period, prompt-injection filtering, a kill switch.
Evaluation
A fixed evaluation set in both languages, run before each release: grounded answers with citations, refusal tests for every out-of-scope category, Arabic quality review by native speakers. Proposed release thresholds: at least 95% of in-scope answers carry a correct citation (the floor used in the NEXUS concept), and 100% refusal on the security red-team set.
Data
CorpusDocument, AdvisorSession, AdvisorTurn (retained for a limited period), EvalRun, RefusalPolicy.
Acceptance
Asked "What are the RO entry requirements?", Dalil answers with the three REG-17 routes and cites REG-17.
Asked about plant security arrangements, Dalil declines and suggests public sources about careers in security instead.
Unauthenticated use is rate-limited and cannot reach staff-mode content.
Disabling Dalil from the admin console removes it from every page within one minute.

Section 11
## Security, privacy and nuclear-information controls
A skills platform for a nuclear operator holds personal data about a workforce that includes licensed operators and security staff. Individually harmless facts can become sensitive in aggregate: a roster of who is qualified for which watch-station, on which shift, is useful to an adversary. Kafa'a is designed so that such information never reaches it, and what does reach it is held to the minimum.
### Data classification
 | Class | Examples | Where it may appear | Controls
 | Public | Role descriptions, programme pages, published statistics with sources, partner directory, competency library | Public site, Dalil public mode | Two-language review; source required; review date
 | Internal | Course content, schedules, curated OE lessons, aggregate analytics | Staff portal by role | Sign-in; role checks; no export without watermark
 | Personal / restricted | Qualification records, licence status, medical due flags, applications, contractor evidence | Own view and scoped roles only | Row-level security; field encryption for sensitive fields; access reviews; retention schedule
 | Excluded | Security arrangements, physical protection, safeguards data, emergency plan details, procedures, plant drawings or layouts, real-time plant data, shift rosters and crew composition, medical content, exam items outside the secure bank | Nowhere in Kafa'a | Content policy, upload scanning for classified markings, Dalil corpus allow-list, pre-publication review
### Controls
People and privacy
No named staff on public pages. Leadership names, if ever needed, link to enec.ae rather than being copied.
Collect the minimum; state the purpose on every form; publish retention periods.
Comply with the UAE Personal Data Protection Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021) and any Abu Dhabi government data rules that apply to ENEC; legal review confirms which regime governs in Phase 0.
Data subject requests through /my/settings, handled by a named privacy role.
No scoring, ranking or profiling of individuals by algorithm.
Platform
Hosting in a UAE cloud region under ENEC-controlled accounts, with data residency in the UAE; keys in a managed service controlled by ENEC.
Access checks in the API layer and again by database row-level security; deny by default.
Append-only audit log for sign-ins, permission changes, record changes, exports and AI administration.
Alignment with ENEC's ISO/IEC 27001 management system and with the cyber security expectations of FANR-REG-08 V2 for anything that touches licensee processes.
Penetration test before each phase goes live; dependency and secret scanning in CI.
### Lessons from uaecb, turned into build gates
 | uaecb finding | Kafa'a gate
 | Public /api/debug/env and /api/ai/diagnostic reveal the OpenAI key prefix and length | CI blocks any route matching debug, diagnostic or test patterns in production; secrets never echo in responses
 | Middleware disabled "to allow demo auth" | Demo access lives in a separate tenant; the production access layer has no bypass flag
 | NEXTAUTH_SECRET placeholder committed in netlify.toml | Secret scanning on every commit; deploys fail on placeholder values
 | 2FA secret stored in plain text | MFA through SSO or UAE Pass; any stored secret encrypted with managed keys
 | Unauthenticated AI chat as an open cost sink | Dalil behind a gateway with quotas, abuse detection and a kill switch
 | Real people scraped into org charts | Automated check against person-name fields in public payloads

## How Kafa'a relates to ENEC NEXUS
ENEC NEXUS is Armie's concept demo of a governed AI-agent operating system for ENEC, modelled on ADGM POLARIS (live demo, build plan, guidebook). Its tagline is "AI informs. A licensed human decides." It covers procurement and supplier qualification, outage support, corrective action, regulatory drafting, fuel, emergency preparedness, sustainability and leadership briefings, with an evidence ledger, a kill switch and an authority matrix of 38 reserved decisions. It deliberately leaves out formal qualification records, individual HR data, public recruitment and university onboarding.
That gap is Kafa'a's scope. NEXUS is the agent operating layer for enterprise work; Kafa'a is the people and skills layer. NEXUS source code and its database belong to the Claude session that maintains it, so this section is a specification of integration points only. Nothing here changes NEXUS.

## Phased roadmap
Five phases, each ending with exit criteria rather than a date. Effort is shown as relative complexity on a four-step scale to , compared with the other phases.
Phase 0Discovery and alignment
Scope. Confirm sponsorship and ownership inside ENEC (Human Capital, Nuclear Training, Supply Chain); agree with the NEXUS owner on shared tables and identity; legal review of data protection regime and hosting; inventory ENEC's existing HR, training-records and learning systems and decide which stays the system of record.
Deliverables. Signed-off role and competency seed (the 28 roles); content policy for excluded information; RBAC matrix approved; integration map; Arabic glossary owner named; KPI baselines.
Dependencies. Access to ENEC stakeholders; NEXUS owner's availability.
Exit criteria. A named product owner and data owner per module; written decision on systems of record; agreed list of public sources; security architecture approved.
Phase 1MVP: public site
Scope. M01 Career Map, M02 public competency library, M06 programme pages and light applications (or link-out), M09 public directory and /suppliers, M12 community and ESG, M13 Dalil public mode, M14 applicant accounts, M15 full Arabic, M16 content and audit core.
Deliverables. Public site in both languages; Dalil with evaluation report; accessibility and penetration test reports.
Dependencies. Phase 0 content approvals; hosting account; translation capacity.
Exit criteria. Every public statistic sourced; zero person-name fields in public payloads; Dalil meets the citation and refusal thresholds; Arabic parity 100%; no open high-severity security findings.
Phase 2Staff portal
Scope. Corporate SSO; M02 qualification records (mirror), M03 licensing tracker (mirror), M04 catalogue and enrolment, M05 assessments including the secure item bank, team views for managers, M08 safety culture hub with the speak-up pointer, development plans.
Deliverables. Integrations with HR, training records, shift roster (counts only) and licensing records; automated RBAC test suite; JPM evaluator app pilot with one discipline.
Dependencies. System-of-record interfaces; ECP approval of the speak-up page; Nuclear Training sign-off on item security.
Exit criteria. Mirror accuracy reconciled against source for a pilot population; RBAC suite passes for all 15 roles; no unflagged stale licence status in a parallel-run period; licensed operators and coordinators accept the tracker in user testing.
Phase 3Partners and workforce
Scope. Partner portal (M09 onboarding, academy, contractor equivalency, ICV), M10 universities and research, M07 workforce and Emiratization analytics, observer reports, NEXUS hand-off to the partner lens and ledger writes.
Deliverables. Partner admin tooling; university consent flows; analytics with suppression; first released observer report.
Dependencies. NEXUS owner agreement on hand-off and ledger; procurement process owners; university agreements.
Exit criteria. Cross-partner access tests pass; equivalency decisions recorded end to end for a pilot contractor; analytics reconcile with GRI disclosures for the same period.
Phase 4Future workforce
Scope. M11 advanced-reactor skills and skill bridges; scenario planner (SIMULATED); Dalil staff and partner modes; decision on whether Kafa'a becomes the system of record for qualifications; deeper ledger integration.
Deliverables. Future-skills content reviewed by ENEC's ADVANCE team; staff-mode Dalil evaluation; system-of-record readiness assessment if pursued.
Dependencies. ENEC strategy decisions on new technology; regulator expectations for any change in records systems.
Exit criteria. No public page implies a deployment decision; staff-mode Dalil passes red-team tests on secure items and personal data; records-system decision documented.

Section 15
## Risks and open questions
### Risks
 | Risk | Severity | Mitigation
 | Sensitive information leaks through aggregation (rosters, crew qualification patterns) | High | Excluded data class; no roster import beyond counts; suppression; security review of every new data feed
 | Users treat the licensing tracker as authoritative | High | "As recorded in" labels, stale-sync behaviour, no sends to FANR, coordinator ownership of every notice
 | Duplicating ENEC systems of record | Medium | Mirror-first design; Phase 0 decision on ownership
 | Overlap or conflict with NEXUS | Medium | Integration points agreed with its owner; no edits to NEXUS from this project
 | Dalil gives an unsafe or wrong answer | Medium | Grounding, refusal policy, evaluation gates, kill switch, human hand-off
 | Public figures go stale or conflict | Medium | Source and "as of" date on every figure; review dates; reconciliation notes
 | Arabic quality lags English | Medium | Parity as a release gate; glossary owner; native review
 | Partner data cross-visibility | High | Row-level security tests per partner; lesson from NEXUS B-06
 | Perception that the concept is an official ENEC site | Medium | "Concept" marking on every page; no ENEC logo; link to enec.ae as the authority
### Open questions
Which ENEC system holds training and qualification records today, and would ENEC ever designate Kafa'a as the record?
Who owns the platform inside ENEC: Human Capital, Nuclear Training, or a shared board?
Should applications run in Kafa'a, or should it link out to ENEC's existing careers channel?
Does FANR have any expectation about third-party systems that mirror operator certification status?
Which data protection regime applies to ENEC as an ADQ-owned Abu Dhabi entity, and does any federal or Abu Dhabi rule require specific hosting?
Will the NEXUS owner treat eco_courses and eco_enrolments as shared or hand them to Kafa'a?
Is there an appetite for a sector-wide platform (FANR, universities, suppliers) like IBF for banking, or should Kafa'a stay ENEC-centred?
### What we could not verify
Headcount and Emiratization: "over 3,000, 60% Emirati" (press) against 2,979 and 54.7% (GRI).
Certified operators: "over 300 certifications, 140 UAE" may count renewals; no public SRO/RO split by nationality.
COIT length (91 or 82 weeks); COCT (6 or about 5 weeks per crew per year); GDP length (18-36 or 12-30 months).
DNT start (2023 or 2024) and first cohort size (20 or 30 recruited).
Number of full-scope simulators (2 at the STC or 4 in total).
Whether Barakah has a separate Shift Technical Advisor role, and the exact Shift Supervisor structure.
INPO/ACAD-style accreditation of Barakah training programmes: no public evidence.
Exact emergency planning zone radii (public pages differ).
KINGS, KAIST, the 2019 second operator group, Energy Pioneers 2017-18 figures and the MoHESR scholarship partnership come from search digests only.
ENEC Consulting's ownership percentage; the date of the ENEC board chair change.
No ENEC-Microsoft agreement, no "Energy Alliance", no signed data-centre power contract and no completed equity stake abroad were found.
